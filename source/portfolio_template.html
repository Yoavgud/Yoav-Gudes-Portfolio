MIS Capacitor Fabrication & Electrical Characterization

NE 343 — Microfabrication & Thin-Film Technology · Fabrication Lab · University of Waterloo

Working with two lab partners, I fabricated metal–insulator–semiconductor (MIS) capacitors from scratch on a 3-inch p-type silicon wafer: RCA clean, PECVD silicon nitride deposition, UV photolithography, reactive-ion etching to pattern the dielectric, DC-sputtered aluminum metallization, a second lithography step, and a wet etch to define the final contacts.

We cross-checked the nitride thickness three ways — colour-chart estimation, ellipsometry, and DEKTAK profilometry — and the mismatch became a result in itself: DEKTAK read about 80% thicker than the other two methods, traced to over-etching into the silicon substrate during RIE. Ellipsometry (104.7 nm) and the colour chart (103.4 nm) agreed closely and became our reference value. Four-point probe measurements across five points on the wafer showed aluminum sheet resistance tightly clustered between 0.0065–0.0072 Ω, indicating uniform metallization.

Electrically, the sweep below — measured on a 300 µm contact — shows the classic MIS signature: capacitance falling as the device moves from accumulation into depletion, then recovering into inversion. I–V measurements across three contact diameters stayed sub-nanoamp out to 20 V, consistent with a low-leakage dielectric.

Diode Characterization & Analog Waveform Shaping

Circuits Labs & Oscilloscope · University of Waterloo

Benchwork with a function generator and a 100 MHz digital oscilloscope: driving triangle and sine inputs through biased clipping and rectifying stages and reading the results live, then pulling a full forward-bias diode I–V curve directly in the scope's XY mode — the same exponential turn-on knee you'd get from the diode equation, traced in real time instead of calculated.

School

Quantum-Confined Photoluminescence

Nanomaterials Lab · University of Waterloo

Six CdSe quantum dot samples, synthesized at increasing nanocrystal size, shift emission from green through orange to deep red under UV — a direct, visible demonstration of quantum confinement: as the crystal shrinks toward the exciton Bohr radius, the effective bandgap widens and the emitted photon energy climbs. Under ambient light the same vials show almost no colour difference; the effect only shows up once you excite the dots directly.
